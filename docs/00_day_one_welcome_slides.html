Section 4

Meet Dr. Reed — Ask Me Anything

Open live to camera here: who you are, your background, and why you teach this course the way you do. Then move into the scripted Q&A slides that follow.
"I made it through ISM2411, but this looks like a big jump — should I be worried?"
It's a real jump, and that's intentional — ISM2411 taught you to script, this course teaches you to build. But it's designed to walk you through it step by step: Unit 1 rebuilds your developer habits before any new Python, and every OOP/database concept gets a small example before it shows up in the capstone.
If Unit 1 feels slow, that's the point — it's building the foundation Units 2–4 lean on hard.
"What's the best way to reach you, and how fast will you respond?"
Canvas Mail or your USF email, with your name, U Number, section, and reason in the first sentence. For code questions, also attach your GitHub link and the complete traceback. 48 business-hour response window; Friday-evening emails get answered by end of day Monday.
Incomplete messages — missing GitHub link or traceback — get returned without a diagnosis. This is by design, not an inconvenience.
"What if I fall behind on the capstone?"
Tell me the moment you notice it, not in Week 16. The capstone is built so each week assumes the last one is done — proposal/schema (13), database (14), interface (15), AI feature + demo (16). If Week 13 slips, come to office hours immediately — that's the only week with room to recover.
This is the single most important warning to give on Day One for this course — capstone weeks compound fast.
"What's your grading philosophy?"
I grade what's actually in your Git log and your submitted repo — the Developer Workflow grade specifically rewards evidence of process, not just a working final product. Ten commits across a week tells a different story than one commit at 11:59 PM, and it's graded that way on purpose.
One week from when a grade posts to raise a dispute — after that, it's final.
"Office hours — when, and what should I bring?"
Monday and Wednesday, 1:30–3:00 PM, in CIS 2070B. Can't make it in person? Check Canvas for the Teams link. Bring your GitHub link, the complete traceback, and what you already tried.
Repeat the room number and days out loud — this is the single most-forgotten detail from Day One.
